Topic : 
Forecasting Value at Risk by Machine Learning Methods

Why and its relevance to our major ?
VaR is the maximum loss a portfolio is expected to suffer over a given horizon at a given confidence level (e.g. at 99 %, the loss should exceed the VaR only 1 day out of 100). Regulations require banks to calculate a Value at Risk (VaR) on a daily basis, and their regulatory capital depends on this figure. To compute this VaR, banks predominantly employ a historical simulation method, typically covering a window of 250 to 500 trading days (roughly 1 to 2 years). Historical simulation assumes the recent past represents the future: it reacts slowly to volatility changes and tends to underestimate risk at the start of crises. There is no consensus in the scientific literature regarding the value that machine learning (ML) might add to such analyses; consequently, determining this is precisely the objective of this project using quantile-regression machine learning model. Market risk measurement is a core task of financial engineering, and this project combines quantitative finance, econometrics and machine learning, the main skills of our major.

Problem statement : 
Can a quantile-regression Machine Learning model forecast the 1-day 99 % Value at Risk of a stock index more accurately than historical simulation, the industry-standard baseline — and does the gain come from the model or from the features ?
